# Aerospace Research: Spacecraft Hardware (JWST, Voyager, ISS, TDRS & Gateway)

## 1. James Webb Space Telescope (JWST)
- **Mission Agency**: NASA in partnership with ESA and CSA.
- **Operational Location**: Sun-Earth L2 Lagrange Point (~1.5 million km from Earth in deep space halo orbit).
- **Physical Architecture & Scale**:
  - Primary Mirror: 6.5 m (21.3 ft) diameter consisting of 18 hexagonal beryllium mirror segments coated with a 100-nanometer layer of vapor-deposited gold ($48.25\text{ g}$ total gold) and covered with a protective layer of amorphous silicon dioxide ($SiO_2$).
  - Hexagonal Mirror Segment Size: 1.32 m flat-to-flat, 20 kg per segment with 6 cryogenic stepper motor actuators for nanometer-precision figure alignment.
  - Secondary Mirror: Circular convex mirror (0.74 m diameter) mounted on a deployable 3-strut tripod mast.
  - Sunshield: 5-layer Kapton membrane ($21.2\text{ m} \times 14.2\text{ m}$ / tennis-court sized) with vapor-deposited aluminum and doped-silicon coatings. Attenuates solar radiation from 383 K (110°C / 230°F) on the hot side down to <50 K (-223°C / -370°F) on the cold science telescope side.
  - Instruments: NIRCam (Near-Infrared Camera), NIRSpec (Near-Infrared Spectrograph), MIRI (Mid-Infrared Instrument), FGS/NIRISS (Fine Guidance Sensor).
- **Mass**: ~6,161 kg at liftoff (Ariane 5 ECA).

## 2. Voyager 1 & 2 Interstellar Probes
- **Mission Agency**: NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL).
- **Status**: Operational in Interstellar Space (>163 AU / 24.4 billion km from Earth for Voyager 1; >136 AU for Voyager 2).
- **Physical Architecture & Scale**:
  - Bus Structure: Decagonal prism (1.78 m across flats) housing propellant tanks, guidance computers, and science instruments.
  - High-Gain Antenna (HGA): 3.7 m (12.0 ft) parabolic Cassegrain dish operating in S-band (2.3 GHz) and X-band (8.4 GHz).
  - Power System: 3 Radioisotope Thermoelectric Generators (RTGs) mounted on a 2.3 m deployable boom containing plutonium-238 ($^{238}\text{Pu}$) dioxide spheres producing ~470 W of electrical power at launch.
  - Science Booms: 13 m (43 ft) fiberglass boom carrying fluxgate magnetometers; steerable scan platform carrying cameras, infrared spectrometers, and cosmic ray detectors.
  - Golden Record: 30 cm gold-plated copper disc containing sounds and images selected to portray the diversity of life on Earth, mounted to the spacecraft bus with an aluminum protective jacket and symbolic playback instructions.
- **Mass**: 722 kg (excluding fuel); 815 kg total at launch.

## 3. International Space Station (ISS)
- **Partnership**: NASA (USA), Roscosmos (Russia), ESA (Europe), JAXA (Japan), CSA (Canada).
- **Orbit**: Low Earth Orbit (LEO) at ~418 km altitude, 51.64° inclination, 92.9 minute orbital period (~27,600 km/h / 7.66 km/s).
- **Physical Architecture & Scale**:
  - Integrated Truss Structure (ITS): 108.5 m (356 ft) overall length spanning port to starboard.
  - Pressurized Length: 72.8 m (239 ft) from front of Harmony to aft of Zvezda.
  - Total Station Mass: ~450,000 kg (~992,000 lb).
  - Living/Habitable Space: ~388 m³ pressurized habitable volume (comparable to a six-bedroom house).
  - Solar Power: 8 Solar Array Wings (SAWs), each 34 m $\times$ 10.7 m, generating 120 kW of continuous electrical power.
  - Thermal Control: 4 pairs of deployable ammonia radiators on the truss rejecting station waste heat into the vacuum of space.
  - Key Modules:
    - Zarya (Functional Cargo Block, 1998)
    - Unity (Node 1)
    - Zvezda (Service Module)
    - Destiny (US Laboratory Module)
    - Quest (Joint Airlock)
    - Columbus (European Laboratory Module)
    - Kibo (Japanese Experiment Module with exposed facility porch)
    - Cupola (7-window observatory with 360-degree Earth nadir views)
    - Canadarm2 (Space Station Remote Manipulator System, 17.6 m articulated robotic arm).

## 4. Tracking and Data Relay Satellites (TDRS)
- **Role**: Replaces fictional space communications relay models with NASA's verified Space Network infrastructure.
- **Orbit**: Geosynchronous Earth Orbit (GEO) at 35,786 km altitude, circular orbit period 23.93 hours.
- **Physical Architecture**:
  - Dual 4.5 m (14.8 ft) steerable mesh reflector dish antennas providing high-bandwidth tracking and relay in S, Ku, and Ka bands.
  - Spacecraft Bus: Boeing 601 / 702 series with solar arrays delivering ~3.2 kW electrical power.
  - Capabilities: Continuous high-data-rate coverage (up to 300 Mbps) for human spaceflight (ISS, Orion), Earth observation satellites (Hubble, Aqua, Terra), and launch vehicle telemetry during ascent over the Atlantic and Pacific oceans.
