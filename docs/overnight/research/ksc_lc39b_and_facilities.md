## 2. Launch Complex 39B (LC-39B) Structure

### 2.1 Pad Mound & Hardstand
- **Elevation**: Pad hardstand sits on an elevated octagonal concrete mound ~12.8 m (42 ft) above mean sea level to protect against storm surges and provide gravity drainage for deluge water.
- **Surface**: Reinforced concrete apron surrounded by perimeter asphalt roadways and security fencing.
- **Crawlerway Entrance**: Gentle 5% incline ramp ascending the south-southwest face of the mound, enabling the Crawler-Transporter to deposit the Mobile Launcher onto six 1.8-meter-tall steel pedestal mount points.

### 2.2 Flame Trench & Deflector
- **Flame Trench**:
  - Dimensions: 137 m (450 ft) long, 15.2 m (50 ft) wide, and 12.8 m (42 ft) deep.
  - Orientation: Bisects the pad along a North-South axis, discharging hot exhaust northward away from the umbilical tower.
  - Lining: Over 90,000 heat-resistant refractory ceramic bricks rated for temperatures exceeding 1,900°C (3,500°F).
- **Flame Deflector**:
  - Main Deflector: Inverted V-shaped steel structure (apex ~10.5 m high) weighing 450 metric tons (1.0 million lbs).
  - Purpose: Splits the 39 MN (8.8 million lbf) exhaust plume from the twin SRBs and four RS-25 hydrolox engines, directing 60% north out the main trench and 40% south through the secondary trench.

### 2.3 Ignition Overpressure & Sound Suppression (IOP/SS) Water System
- **Elevated Water Tower**:
  - Height: 88.4 m (290 ft) total height.
  - Capacity: 1,135,000 liters (300,000 gallons) of fresh water.
  - Flow Rate: Discharges up to 4,160,000 L/min (1,100,000 gpm) under pure gravity head through dual 2.1-meter (7-foot) diameter carbon steel penstocks.
  - Activation: Water release begins at T-16 seconds. Water cascades over the flame deflector and mobile launcher deck at 340,000 gallons per minute, creating a dense acoustic attenuation blanket that reduces acoustic energy by 6 to 10 dB, preventing acoustic shockwaves from reflecting off the concrete and damaging the vehicle payload.

### 2.4 Lightning Protection System (LPS)
- **Geometry**: Unlike the single lightning mast used during the Apollo and Shuttle eras, NASA constructed three massive steel lattice towers forming an equilateral triangle surrounding LC-39B.
- **Tower Dimensions**:
  - Height: 182.9 m (600 ft) tall per tower.
  - Distance: Each tower is located ~180 m from the center of the pad.
  - Overhead Catenary Wires: Overhead fiberglass mast insulators and heavy braided steel ground wires link the tower peaks, creating an electrical Faraday-cage umbrella over the 111-meter-tall SLS and Mobile Launcher stack.
  - Lighting: Strobe beacons and FAA red obstruction lamps at 45 m, 90 m, 135 m, and 183 m.

## 3. Mobile Launcher 1 (ML-1) Details

- **Base Structure**:
  - Dimensions: 7.6 m high, 41.8 m long, 39.6 m wide.
  - Exhaust Holes: Central core stage exhaust hole (10.7 m × 9.8 m) and two solid rocket booster exhaust holes (6.1 m × 4.9 m).
- **Umbilical Tower**:
  - Height: 108.2 m (355 ft) above the launcher base (total height ~115.8 m above pad).
  - Steel Truss: Heavy structural steel box truss with internal personnel elevator, emergency escape chutes (slidewires), and cryogenic pipe risers (liquid hydrogen, liquid oxygen, gaseous nitrogen, gaseous helium).
- **Service Umbilicals & Swing Arms**:
  1. **Crew Access Arm (CAA)**:
     - Level: 274 ft level (~83.5 m).
     - Span: 20 m articulated steel truss swinging 120° into position.
     - Environmental Enclosure ("White Room"): Cleanroom at the distal end sealing against the Orion capsule hatch. Retracts prior to launch (T-7 minutes).
  2. **Orion Service Module Umbilical (OSMU)**:
     - Level: ~70 m level.
     - Function: Supplies chilled environmental purge air, coolant fluid, and electrical power to the European Service Module (ESM).
  3. **Interim Cryogenic Propulsion Stage Umbilical (ICPSU)**:
     - Level: ~60 m level.
     - Function: Supplies liquid hydrogen/oxygen topping lines and boil-off vent to the ICPS RL10B-2 upper stage.
  4. **Core Stage Forward Skirt Umbilical (CSFSU)**:
     - Level: ~45 m level.
     - Function: Avionics conditioning, GN2 purge, and flight computer communications.
  5. **Core Stage Inter-Tank Umbilical (CSITU)**:
     - Level: ~34 m level.
     - Function: Liquid oxygen fill/drain vent lines between the LH2 and LOX tanks.
  6. **Tail Service Mast Umbilicals (TSMUs)**:
     - Deck Level: Two massive steel masts (10 m tall) on the launcher deck flanking the core stage aft skirt.
     - TSMU-LH2 (Left): Feeds 1,260 gpm liquid hydrogen at -253°C.
     - TSMU-LOX (Right): Feeds 1,000 gpm liquid oxygen at -183°C.
     - Disconnect: Quick-disconnect plates tilt back with protective steel roll-up hoods at T-0 liftoff.

## 4. Surrounding Kennedy Space Center Geography

### 4.1 Vehicle Assembly Building (VAB) & Launch Control Center (LCC)
- **VAB Dimensions**:
  - Height: 160.3 m (526 ft) — one of the highest volume single-story buildings on Earth.
  - Length: 218.2 m (716 ft), Width: 157.9 m (518 ft).
  - Exterior: Painted steel cladding with distinctive vertical red/white/blue flag stripes and high-bay roll-up doors (139 m tall).
- **Launch Control Center (LCC)**:
  - 4-story command building adjoining the east side of the VAB, with large canted green glass observation windows facing northeast toward LC-39B.

### 4.2 Crawlerway
- **Layout**: Two parallel 12.2 m (40 ft) wide crushed river stone tracks separated by a 15.2 m (50 ft) grass median (total width 39.6 m).
- **Material**: Tumbled Alabama river stone gravel (approx. 10–20 cm depth) over compacted limestone bed, designed to crush slightly under the 8,200 metric ton combined weight of the Crawler and loaded Mobile Launcher without sparking.
- **Route**: Sweeps southwest from LC-39B for 6.8 km, joining the LC-39A spur before terminating at the VAB high-bay doors.

### 4.3 Florida Space Coast Landscape & Flora
- **Terrain**: Very flat barrier island topography (Merritt Island National Wildlife Refuge) ranging from 0 to 4 meters elevation.
- **Vegetation**:
  - Sabal palmetto (Florida cabbage palm) clusters.
  - Slash pine (Pinus elliottii) scrub and salt-tolerant coastal scrub oaks.
  - Mangrove wetlands (black and red mangroves) along the brackish lagoon shorelines.
- **Atmospheric Appearance**:
  - Humid subtropical marine boundary layer: characteristic low-altitude aerosol haze (Rayleigh & Mie scattering) softening distant horizons.
  - Horizon sea-line: Atlantic Ocean to the East with soft surf break; coastal dunes rising 2–3 m above high water mark.
