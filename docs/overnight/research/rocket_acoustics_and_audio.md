# Verified Aerospace Research: Rocket Acoustics, Launch Audio & Multi-Layer Sound Synthesis

## 1. Acoustic Physics of Super-Heavy Launch Vehicles
Rocket engine acoustic energy is not a static noise loop; it is generated by turbulent supersonic shear layers and combustion processes:
1. **Total Acoustic Power**:
   - Typically between $0.5\%$ and $1.0\%$ of total jet kinetic power ($P_{jet} = \frac{1}{2} \dot{m} v_e^2$ or $\approx \frac{1}{2} T v_e$).
   - For SLS Block 1 liftoff thrust ($T \approx 39.14 \text{ MN}$, $v_e \approx 2,700 \text{ m/s}$ average):
     $$P_{jet} \approx \frac{1}{2} \times (39.14 \times 10^6 \text{ N}) \times 2,700 \text{ m/s} \approx 52.8 \text{ GW}$$
     $$P_{acoustic} \approx 0.007 \times 52.8 \text{ GW} \approx 370 \text{ MW}$$
   - Sound pressure level at LC-39B mobile launcher deck exceeds 175–185 dB OASPL (overall sound pressure level), requiring 1.7 million liters/min sound suppression water deluge to protect the vehicle from acoustic structural fatigue.

2. **Spectral Components & Acoustic Layers**:
   - **Layer 1: Sub-bass Rumble (25–85 Hz)**:
     - Driven by large-scale turbulent eddies in the outer exhaust plume and low-frequency chamber pressure fluctuations.
     - Attenuates slowly over distance; felt deeply in the chest rather than heard.
   - **Layer 2: Supersonic Shear Crackle (800–4,000 Hz)**:
     - First mathematically described by Alan Powell and Ffowcs Williams. Supersonic exhaust jets ($M_j > 2.5$) generate asymmetric Mach waves that coalesce into acoustic shock fronts.
     - Results in sharp, tearing, popcorn-like crackle with high skewness of sound pressure derivative ($S > 0.4$).
     - Varies with atmospheric density and throttle.
   - **Layer 3: Solid Rocket Booster Roar (120–800 Hz)**:
     - Burning of hydroxyl-terminated polybutadiene (HTPB) with ammonium perchlorate and aluminum produces burning aluminum oxide ($Al_2O_3$) particulate droplets.
     - Imparts a distinct raspy, gritty, high-density acoustic profile compared to clean hydrolox gas.
   - **Layer 4: Aerodynamic Buffeting / Boundary Layer Noise**:
     - Peaks near Maximum Dynamic Pressure (Max-Q, altitude 10–14 km, dynamic pressure ~35 kPa).
     - Fades exponentially above 35 km as atmospheric density $\rho$ decreases:
       $$p_{dynamic} = \frac{1}{2} \rho v^2$$
   - **Layer 5: In-Cabin Structural Transmission**:
     - Inside the pressurized Orion or Apollo Command Module, airborne acoustic energy is attenuated by ~25–35 dB by the titanium/aluminum pressure shell and thermal insulation.
     - Mechanical vibrations propagate through thrust pucks and mounting struts, presenting a muffled low-pass profile (~350–450 Hz cutoff) with internal avionics pump whir.

3. **Distance Propagation & Sound Delay**:
   - Speed of sound in ambient air ($T \approx 20^\circ\text{C}$): $c_s \approx 343 \text{ m/s}$.
   - Time delay:
     $$\Delta t = \frac{d}{c_s}$$
     - At pad viewing distance of 5.5 km (Banana River site), the sound arrives ~16 seconds after ignition flame is visible.
     - In-game camera tracking distances (e.g. 50 m to 1,500 m) exhibit delay of 0.15 s to 4.4 s.
   - High-Frequency Air Absorption:
     - High frequencies attenuate faster due to viscous friction and molecular relaxation of $N_2$ and $O_2$. Low frequencies (<100 Hz) propagate for tens of kilometers.

4. **Doppler Effect**:
   - When ascending at velocity $v_r$ along the camera line of sight:
     $$f' = f \left(1 - \frac{v_r}{c_s}\right)$$
